The English Renaissance

From Medieval Theocentrism to Tudor Humanism, the Birth of English Poetics, and Christopher Marlowe’s Faustian Titanism.

1. Historical Context: The Paradigm Shift (Warm-Up)

Cultural Shift: Theocentrism to Anthropocentrism

The English Renaissance marked a radical transition from the medieval theocentric model (wherein earthly existence was viewed primarily as a fleeting trial subordinate to post-mortem salvation and ecclesiastical authority) to civic humanism and anthropocentrism (which elevated human potential, empirical enquiry, and terrestrial legacy).

Triggered by the arrival of the printing press in Westminster (William Caxton, 1476) and accelerated by Henry VIII’s Reformation, the vernacular English tongue was transformed from a coarse provincial dialect into a versatile vessel capable of intellectual discourse, theological debate, and high aesthetic experimentation.

The Iconography of Discovery: Armillary Spheres & Cabinets of Curiosities

During Elizabeth I's reign, courtiers were portrayed not with relics of saints, but surrounded by cartographic globes, celestial astrolabes, and mathematical instruments. The Renaissance Wunderkammer (cabinet of curiosities) manifested a voracious imperial appetite to categorize, decipher, and conquer both nature and classical antiquity.

3. Close Reading: Lyric, Allegory, and Tragedy

From Italian Borrowing to National Epic

Thomas Wyatt & Henry Howard, Earl of Surrey: Introduced the Petrarchan model to England. Italian sonnets relied on an octave ($abba\ abba$) followed by a sestet ($cde\ cde$). Because English contains fewer rhyming word-endings than Italian, Surrey adapted the rhyme scheme to three quatrains and a concluding rhyming couplet ($abab\ cdcd\ efef\ gg$), generating an epigrammatic punchline at the end.

Edmund Spenser: Composed The Faerie Queene, marrying Arthurian chivalry with Christian Protestant allegory and political panegyric for Elizabeth I (personified as the divine maiden Gloriana). He invented the 9-line Spenserian Stanza: eight iambic pentameters concluded by an Alexandrine (iambic hexameter, 12 syllables) rhyming $ababbcbcc$.

Text Fragment A: Thomas Wyatt — Whoso List to Hunt (c. 1530s)

Whoso list to hunt, I know where is an hind,
But as for me, hélas, I may no more.
The vain travail hath wearied me so sore,
I am of them that farthest cometh behind.
Yet may I by no means my wearied mind
Draw from the deer, but as she fleeth afore
Fainting I follow. I leave off therefore,
Sithens in a net I seek to hold the wind.
Who list her hunt, I put him out of doubt,
As well as I may spend his time in vain.
And graven with diamonds in letters plain
There is written, her fair neck round about:
Noli me tangere, for Caesar’s I am,
And wild for to hold, though I seem tame.

Text Fragment B: Christopher Marlowe — Doctor Faustus (The Final Soliloquy)

Stand still, you ever-moving spheres of heaven,
That time may cease, and midnight never come!
Fair Nature’s eye, rise, rise again, and make
Perpetual day; or let this hour be but
A year, a month, a week, a natural day,
That Faustus may repent and save his soul!
O lente, lente currite noctis equi!
The stars move still, time runs, the clock will strike,
The devil will come, and Faustus must be damned.
O, I’ll leap up to my God! Who pulls me down?
See, see, where Christ’s blood streams in the firmament!

4. Grammar Theory & Discourse Syntax: Inversion & Cleft Sentences

Stylistic Emphasis: Negative Inversion & Subjunctive Constructions

To convey dramatic urgency, philosophical solemnity, and elevate written register (essential for IELTS Academic Band 8.0+), early modern and formal academic writers employ Negative / Restrictive Inversion.

Formula: Negative Adverbial (Seldom, Scarcely, No sooner, Not only) + Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Main Verb

Example: "Hardly had Faustus signed the demonic covenant when dread consumed him."

Cleft Sentences for Thematic Focus: It was + [Focus] + that/who... or What + [Clause] + was/is...
Example: "What terrified early modern audiences was not the devil's appearance, but the protagonist's refusal of grace."
